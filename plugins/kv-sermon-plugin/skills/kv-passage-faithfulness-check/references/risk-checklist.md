# Risk Checklist

Use these cues to identify recurring passage-faithfulness problems.

## Forced framework
A framework becomes controlling instead of the passage. Watch for divine council, sacred space, Babel/nations/Pentecost, territorial spirits, covenant categories, kingdom theology, typology, prophecy, and eschatology language imported without textual warrant.

## Unsupported inference
A possible implication is stated as a certainty. Watch for "proves," "clearly means," "always," "never," or "the real reason" when support is limited.

## Moralism
The output reduces the passage to self-improvement, virtue, effort, or behavior detached from God's action, character, covenant faithfulness, kingdom, or gospel fulfillment.

## Prosperity / transactional framing
The output implies obedience, faith, generosity, prayer, or repentance guarantees material success, safety, health, influence, visible results, or storm-free living when the passage does not teach that.

## Therapeutic drift
The output turns the passage mainly into self-esteem, dream discovery, emotional relief, or personal fulfillment without grounding in the text's own burden.

## Fear, shame, or manipulation
The output pressures the hearer beyond the passage's own force or uses guilt, threat, or emotional leverage to manufacture response.

## Flattened gospel connection
The output jumps to Jesus or the gospel in a way that ignores the passage's genre, structure, tension, or contribution. Christ-centered synthesis should be organic, not pasted on.

## Original-language overclaiming
The output claims secret meanings, root fallacies, grammar-based certainties that do not affect interpretation, or decorative word studies.

## Observation / interpretation leakage
A question labeled observation already tells learners what to interpret. Observation prompts should ask what the text says, repeats, contrasts, commands, depicts, or emphasizes before asking what it means.

## Background as textual proof
Historical, cultural, archaeological, or geographical background is treated as if it were explicitly stated in the passage. Background may illuminate, but it must not control the text without support.
