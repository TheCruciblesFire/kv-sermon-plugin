# Support Levels

Use these categories consistently when auditing claims.

## Direct textual support
The passage explicitly states the claim or clearly depicts it in the narrative, poem, prophecy, argument, command, or discourse.

Handling: keep the claim. Strengthen wording only if the draft is vague.

## Strong contextual inference
The claim is not word-for-word in the passage, but the immediate context, structure, genre, repeated language, argument flow, or narrative logic strongly supports it.

Handling: keep with careful wording. Avoid saying "the text proves" when "the text supports" is more accurate.

## Responsible biblical-theological synthesis
The claim connects the passage to broader Scripture responsibly, but the connection is not the local passage's direct or primary claim.

Handling: present proportionately. Say "within the broader biblical storyline" or "this connects with" rather than "this passage teaches" if the local text does not directly state it.

## Weak support / possible overreach
The claim may be true in Christian theology or elsewhere in Scripture, but it is not well grounded in the controlling passage.

Handling: revise, qualify, relocate, or support from a more appropriate passage.

## Speculation / distortion
The claim contradicts, manipulates, sensationalizes, flattens, or outruns the passage. It may promise what the text does not promise or impose a framework the text does not support.

Handling: remove or replace. Provide safer wording rooted in the passage.
