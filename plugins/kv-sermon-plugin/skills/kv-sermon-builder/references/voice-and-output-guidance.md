# Voice and Output Guidance

Use this reference for full manuscripts, hybrid manuscripts, speaking outlines, and substantial revisions.

## Core Voice

Aim for preaching that is:

- pastoral;
- clear;
- text-governed;
- theologically responsible;
- spiritually serious;
- accessible to lay listeners;
- conversational where appropriate;
- direct without harshness;
- warm without sentimentality;
- practical without shallowness;
- weighty without inflation.

## Write for the Ear

Prefer sentences that are clear, speakable, and easy to follow aloud.

Use natural contractions when they improve spoken flow.

Avoid:

- long academic sentences;
- stacked abstractions;
- unnecessary jargon;
- filler;
- cliches;
- hype;
- slang;
- sentimental padding;
- cleverness that distracts from the passage.

## Manuscript Style

A full manuscript should sound preached, not merely written. Default to **connected narrative paragraphs** rather than stacked one-line sentences. The manuscript should read like sustained spoken thought, not like a slide deck or teleprompter built from fragments.

Use:

- clear movement from text to truth to response;
- smooth transitions;
- concrete application;
- strong but natural conclusions;
- paragraphs normally around 3-6 sentences, with varied sentence length and natural oral cadence;
- short standalone sentences only when they provide intentional emphasis, a memorable refrain, a transition, or a climactic application.

Avoid as the default prose rhythm:

- one sentence per paragraph;
- sentence fragments stacked vertically;
- successive imperative lines that could be combined into a paragraph;
- repeated `Not X. Not Y. Not Z.` constructions;
- turning ordinary exposition into bullet-like cadence.

Use a cadence check: repeated one-sentence paragraphs are a revision signal unless they are functioning as an intentional refrain, contrast, question, climax, Scripture block, or true list. When several short statements advance one thought, consolidate them into a coherent paragraph unless rhetorical separation materially improves delivery.

## Hybrid Manuscript

Treat a hybrid manuscript as **manuscript prose with selective structural or preacher cues**. Do not treat it as a manuscript/outline blend, and do not label it "hybrid manuscript / preaching outline" unless the user explicitly requests both. Developed exposition, theological significance, application, transitions, and conclusion prose must visually and rhetorically resemble a manuscript. Selective cues may mark headings, Scripture, true lists, questions, transitions, or preacher reminders.

Outside Scripture blocks, headings, true lists, response questions, deliberate refrains, and clearly intentional rhetorical emphasis, repeated one-sentence paragraphs should trigger revision. When several related short statements advance one thought, consolidate them into a coherent paragraph.

This is the preferred middle form between a skeletal outline and a word-for-word manuscript: **manuscript prose with selective cues**, not outline cadence with occasional prose. Line breaks should mark real paragraph or structural boundaries, not substitute for vocal pacing.

## Mandatory Two-Pass Prose Normalization

Do not try to solve manuscript rhythm while drafting sentence by sentence. First complete the sermon faithfully. Then perform a separate editorial pass using `prose-normalization-workflow.md`.

During that second pass, treat every blank line as a real paragraph boundary. Merge adjacent ordinary one-sentence paragraphs whenever they belong to the same argument unit, even if they are not repetitive or parallel. A claim followed by its explanation, qualification, consequence, or application should normally stay in the same developed paragraph unless the thought genuinely turns. Convert stacked imperatives or fragments into ordinary spoken sentences, and retain standalone lines only when their isolation clearly improves emphasis. Most exposition, theological explanation, application, and conclusion material should read as sustained multi-sentence prose.

Example of the unwanted pattern:

> Peter begins with Christ.
>
> That matters.
>
> He does not begin with self-improvement.

Normalize it into connected spoken prose:

> Peter begins with Christ, and that matters because he is not offering a generic self-improvement plan. He grounds Christian endurance in belonging to the suffering and victorious Jesus, so the command grows out of identity before it becomes application.

Another unwanted pattern:

> Peter does none of that.
>
> Pray clearly.
>
> Love deeply.
>
> Show hospitality.
>
> Serve one another.

Normalize ordinary explanation around the list:

> Peter does none of that. Instead, his response to the nearness of the end is strikingly ordinary and concrete: believers are to pray with clarity, love one another deeply, practice hospitality, and use what God has given them to serve. His eschatology produces present faithfulness rather than speculative distraction.

The point is not to eliminate rhetorical short sentences. Use them sparingly where separation materially improves emphasis. Do not calculate paragraph percentages during live generation; quantitative thresholds belong only to regression scoring.

## Speaking Outline

Use short memory-jogging phrases, Scripture cues, application reminders, and transition lines. This mode is the primary exception to the narrative-paragraph default.

Preserve the sermon's logic. Do not compress so far that the preacher loses the textual argument.

## Revision

When revising user-supplied material:

- preserve the user's recognizable voice;
- preserve approved Main Idea, Goal, major texts, and series arc unless the user requests a rebuild or passage faithfulness requires correction;
- diagnose scope before rewriting;
- trim repetition before cutting necessary exposition;
- protect the sermon burden, gospel logic, and conclusion during shortening.

## Illustrations

Use illustrations to clarify a movement, not to become the movement.

Avoid manipulative, sensational, unverifiable, or distracting stories.

When factual accuracy matters, verify or frame an illustration generically rather than inventing specifics.

## Output Matching

- Prep orientation: concise and directional.
- Outline: preachably detailed, not manuscripted.
- Hybrid: manuscript prose with selective structural or preacher cues; prose remains dominant.
- Full manuscript: natural spoken prose.
- Speaking outline: memory-jogging delivery cues.
- Revision: change only what was requested unless a broader change is required and explained.
