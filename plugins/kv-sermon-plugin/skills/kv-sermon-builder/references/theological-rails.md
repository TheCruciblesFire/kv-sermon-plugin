# Theological Rails for Sermon Construction

Use this reference when theology, biblical-theological synthesis, interpretive tension, original-language material, or recurring ministry themes materially shape the sermon.

## Text First

Begin with the passage's own words, structure, genre, argument, and burden.

Do not begin with a system, theme, illustration, cultural issue, or ministry agenda and then search for textual support.

## Canonical Coherence Without Flattening

Use the wider canon to clarify and deepen the controlling passage, not to bypass it.

Distinguish:

- what the controlling text directly says;
- reasonable inference;
- biblical-theological synthesis;
- application;
- illustration.

## Christ-Centered Preaching

Keep Christian proclamation centered on Christ without forcing Christological readings through loose association, allegory, or wordplay.

Responsible connections may arise through direct promise/fulfillment, covenant development, kingdom hope, human need and divine provision, priest/king/prophet/temple/sacrifice/wisdom/sonship themes, or the larger redemptive storyline.

## Kingdom of God

Kingdom is an important biblical framework, not a universal sermon template.

Use it when the passage materially concerns God's reign, allegiance, Christ's lordship, justice, righteousness, restoration, discipleship, hostile powers, or new creation.

Do not force kingdom language where another burden is primary.

## Covenant

Preserve development across creation, Noah, Abraham, Sinai, David, the New Covenant, and fulfillment in Christ where relevant.

Do not treat every covenant as identical or make covenant connections carry more weight than the text permits.

## Israel, Nations, and Church

Handle continuity and development carefully. Preserve Israel's particularity, the nations motif, Christ's fulfillment, and the multinational people of God where the passage supports those connections.

## Divine Council and Hostile Powers

Use only when textually or canonically warranted.

- Distinguish textual claim from inference.
- Label speculation.
- Do not build the sermon's central burden on uncertain background material.
- Do not force territorial-spirit claims.
- Keep God's sovereignty, Christ's victory, and faithful discipleship central.

## Sacred Space, Temple, and Presence

Use Eden, tabernacle, temple, priesthood, presence, church-as-dwelling, and New Jerusalem connections only when warranted.

Do not make every garden, mountain, city, building, or table into a sacred-space claim.

## Spiritual Formation and the Spirit

When the passage supports it, connect regeneration, indwelling, filling, fruit, gifts, conviction, holiness, witness, and endurance to faithful discipleship.

Do not equate emotional intensity or religious activity with spiritual maturity.

## Eschatological Hope

Center eschatology on Christ, resurrection, judgment, vindication, the defeat of evil, and new creation.

Avoid sensationalism, speculative timelines, or false certainty where faithful interpreters disagree.

## Application Guardrails

Avoid:

- generic moralism;
- prosperity framing;
- shame-heavy pressure without gospel hope;
- therapeutic advice detached from the text;
- political commentary not demanded by the passage;
- applications built on weak cross-references;
- framework-first application.

## Original-Language and Background Restraint

Use Greek, Hebrew, historical background, archaeology, and scholarly claims only when they materially clarify the sermon.

Do not:

- use original languages as decoration;
- claim a lexical item proves more than its context supports;
- present debated background as certainty;
- let background replace the passage's argument.

When original-language verification itself is the primary task, route upstream to Study Engine.
